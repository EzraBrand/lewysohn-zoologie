Ludwig Lewysohn, Die Zoologie des Talmuds

Frankfurt am Main, 1858: the first systematic collection of the Talmudic and Midrashic material on animals, in German (set in Fraktur) with Hebrew, Aramaic, Greek and Latin. This reading edition has the whole book, pp. 1–400 and the front matter, in a machine transcription and English translation, made to see what current tools can do. The companion project for plants is Löw, Die Flora der Juden.

New: Talmud zoology fact-check, the Talmud's statements on animals (and Lewysohn's comments on them) checked against modern zoology, with links to the §§ here.

What this is

  • The transcription and the translation are machine output (transcription: Claude Opus 5.5 and, from p. 64 on, Sonnet 5.5; Hebrew read a second time and corrected by a vote of three checks; translation: Opus 5.5). Two pages (pp. 68 and 70) were checked by hand.
  • The book has no list of abbreviations. They are expanded from a table built from the text and reviewed by hand (table); “guess” marks an expansion that is not certain.
  • Each page links to the scan: please check it before citing anything.
  • Code, data, method and costs: github.com/EzraBrand/lewysohn-zoologie.
